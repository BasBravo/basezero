## An identity for family life

The landing introduces Ohana with the same idea that guides the app: the day, tasks and people belong together. The sky, colored pills and approachable language make family organization feel like part of everyday life. The images below show the Spanish landing.

![Spanish Ohana landing page with its message about the day, tasks and family against a blue sky.](/projects/ohana/case/landing.webp "The landing introduces the product's promise and the app's visual language.")

![Ohana landing composition with the overview on an iPhone surrounded by tasks, notes and finances.](/projects/ohana/case/resumen-landing.webp "The composition gathers the small pieces of family life around the overview.")

There is always something to remember in a family: an appointment, the shopping, a task or a shared expense. Often these things live in different places and depend on someone remembering to share them out.

Ohana brings everyday organization together in an iPhone app. The idea is for everyone to see **what is happening, what needs doing and how they can help**, without having to ask about everything.

![Ohana's iPhone overview showing the family's day and tasks.](/projects/ohana/case/resumen-en.webp "The overview brings what needs attention into one view for each person.")

## One view of the day

The overview opens with a sentence about the day, followed by upcoming events and sections for each tool. The calendar and responsibilities sit together, with the things needing attention first.

![Ohana's weekly calendar with family events.](/projects/ohana/case/calendario-en.webp "The shared calendar shows the week's plans at a glance.")

## Sharing the work at home

Ohana brings together tasks, shopping, routines and finances. Tasks can be assigned to people, and children's tasks can earn points and rewards. There is also room to coordinate shared expenses and goals.

![Ohana's tasks for today showing the family's responsibilities.](/projects/ohana/case/tareas-en.webp "Tasks show what remains to be done and who is responsible.")

Family can take many forms: Ohana lets people create separate spaces for their household, partner, extended family or caring for someone, and share each space with the people involved.

## A calendar with context

The agenda combines family events with dated tasks and iPhone calendars, including Google and Outlook accounts. Week, month and agenda views offer different ways to see the hours or understand what is coming. Each event can bring together the location, attendees and who is accompanying them, so organizing a plan does not depend on a chain of messages.

## Routines, shopping and sharing the load

The shopping list is shared, so what one person adds is available to everyone else. Housework can be organized by zone with rotating turns; routines break repeated moments into steps, with streaks and points. The aim is to make everyday work visible and let each family member take part.

![Ohana's shared shopping list on an iPhone.](/projects/ohana/case/compra-en.webp "The shopping list brings together what the family needs.")

## The plus button: say it in your own words

The landing presents one entry point for adding things without first finding the right tool. A person can type or dictate what they need, and Ohana opens the relevant screen with the information ready to review; it does not save anything on its own. With Apple Intelligence, the phrase is interpreted on the iPhone.

![Spanish Ohana landing section about the plus button, with example phrases on an iPhone.](/projects/ohana/case/asistente-landing.webp "The plus button starts with what someone wants to do and prepares the next step for review.")

## Finances with context

The finances section brings accounts, transactions, budgets and savings goals together. An expense can be split among the people sharing it, and balances show who owes whom. Savings goals give plans such as a trip or a purchase a place of their own, with contributions and a target date.

The landing also describes ways to bring in payments, receipts and bank screenshots: automatically captured items wait for review before being added. This keeps the person in control of the category and the split.

![Ohana's finances overview on an iPhone.](/projects/ohana/case/finanzas-en.webp "Finances brings the family's money and shared goals together.")

## Spaces for different kinds of family

A space can bring together a household, a couple, extended family or people coordinating someone's care. Each one keeps its own context and is shared with the people chosen. Colors help identify each person across the day, tasks and expenses.

![Ohana's spaces screen on an iPhone.](/projects/ohana/case/espacios-en.webp "Spaces keep what is shared with each group separate.")

## Documents close at hand

Ohana provides a place for documents such as passports, insurance and warranties, with expiry dates and reminders. They can be scanned with the camera, kept personal or shared in a space; each record gathers the information needed to find a document and prepare for renewal.

![A family document record in Ohana.](/projects/ohana/case/documentos-en.webp "Each document brings its details, expiry date and reminders together.")

## Ohana without opening the app

Widgets bring tasks, habits and budgets to the Home Screen. A Live Activity shows a routine's progress on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island, with an action to complete the current step. This brings parts of the day closer to where people already are.

![Ohana widgets and a routine on an iPhone Lock Screen, shown on the Spanish landing.](/projects/ohana/case/widgets-landing.webp "Widgets and Live Activities bring tasks and routines to the Home and Lock Screens.")

## Privacy by design

Family information lives on its members' devices and in their iCloud accounts. According to Ohana's website, app data is not stored on Ohana servers, and there are no ads or third-party analytics.

![Ohana's privacy settings on an iPhone.](/projects/ohana/case/privacidad-en.webp "Privacy is part of the proposition: family data stays in iCloud.")
